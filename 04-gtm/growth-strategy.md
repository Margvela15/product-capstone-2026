# Growth Strategy

**Team:** Undefined Behaviour  
**Product:** KIU Nexus -- a unified academic command center that helps KIU students manage fragmented academic updates from multiple platforms.  
**Document version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-05-08

---

## 1. Target User (Reference)

The primary users are third-year KIU Computer Science students who manage coursework across Moodle, Microsoft Teams, Messenger, Discord, and WhatsApp. They struggle with scattered academic updates, missed requirements, and the time cost of manually checking multiple platforms every day.

---

## 2. Activation Metric

The aha moment happens when a student acknowledges a critical academic update and adds it to their weekly plan.

**Aha moment:** User clicks **Acknowledge and Add to Plan** on a critical academic update.

**Activation metric:** At least 60% of users who sign up acknowledge one critical update within their first session.

**Example:** "User acknowledges a critical update within the first session. Activation = 60% of signups acknowledge an update during onboarding or first dashboard visit."

---

## 3. Three Acquisition Channels

### Channel 1 -- KIU Messenger Groups

**Type:** Organic

**Why this channel:**
- **Fit:** KIU students already coordinate coursework and announcements through Messenger groups.
- **Speed:** Can begin immediately within 1 to 2 days.
- **Cost:** Free aside from team outreach time.

**What we will do in Sprint 2:**  
Post onboarding demos, screenshots, and productivity-focused explanations in KIU class groups and project chats. Ask bridge/asker students to share the product in their own academic groups.

**What success looks like in 4 weeks:**  
100 visitors, 40 signups, and at least 20 activated users.

---

### Channel 2 -- Discord and Student Communities

**Type:** Organic / Viral

**Why this channel:**
- **Fit:** Technical students already spend time in Discord servers for projects and teamwork.
- **Speed:** Can begin within one week.
- **Cost:** Very low; mainly team outreach time.

**What we will do in Sprint 2:**  
Share examples of fragmented academic workflows and demonstrate how KIU Nexus reduces manual checking. Encourage early users to invite teammates.

**What success looks like in 4 weeks:**  
60 visitors, 25 signups, and at least 10 referral-based users.

---

### Channel 3 -- Referral Through Bridge/Asker Students

**Type:** Viral

**Why this channel:**
- **Fit:** Interview evidence showed that bridge/asker students already coordinate updates for teammates.
- **Speed:** Referral behavior can begin immediately even before full collaboration features.
- **Cost:** Low cost because users themselves spread the product.

**What we will do in Sprint 2:**  
Track invites and ask early users to onboard teammates into shared academic spaces.

**What success looks like in 4 weeks:**  
At least 30% of active users invite one teammate.

---

## 4. Channel Ranking and Rationale

| Rank | Channel | One-sentence rationale |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | KIU Messenger Groups | Highest fit because target users already rely on Messenger daily for academic coordination. |
| 2 | Referral Through Bridge/Asker Students | Strong long-term growth potential because the workflow naturally spreads between teammates. |
| 3 | Discord and Student Communities | Good supporting acquisition source, but weaker than Messenger for academic communication. |

---

## 5. Channels We Considered and Rejected

**TikTok Ads** -- Rejected because the team lacks budget and short-form entertainment content is unlikely to target serious academic workflow pain effectively.

**Product Hunt Launch** -- Rejected because KIU Nexus is highly local and student-specific rather than a broad SaaS product.

**LinkedIn Promotion** -- Rejected because students do not coordinate coursework through LinkedIn.

---

## 6. Open Questions

What we do not yet know that we need to answer in Sprint 2:

1. Will students prefer invitation-based onboarding or individual onboarding first?
2. Which wording performs better: “Acknowledge” or “Mark as Handled”?
3. Will students trust manually seeded updates before live integrations exist?
